\section{Implementation and Evaluation}
\label{sec:eval}

The impostor Hamming distribution of the code is the single object that drives both accuracy (EER,
collision floor) and communication (the sub-sample count $T$, hence bytes). We diagnose Super-Bit as
the bottleneck and fix it, first with the training-free metric-aware map (R1, \S\ref{sec:ortho-thermo}),
then with the learned quantisation-aware head (R2, \S\ref{sec:featurehead}), on the same frozen CNN and
the same 128-bit FLPSI backend.

\paragraph{Setup.} The extractor is a frozen Blind-Touch~\cite{ChoiWooKim24} Siamese CNN trained on
SOCOFing~\cite{Shehu18} (6,000 fingerprints, 600 subjects $\times$ 10 fingers) with an identity-disjoint
split: 4,800 training images and 1,200 held-out identities absent from every training, calibration, and
head-fitting step. Enrolment uses the \texttt{Real} captures, probes \texttt{Altered-\{Easy,Medium,
Hard\}}; generality is checked on PolyU and FVC2002, held out from all code training. Super-Bit is the
block-orthonormal random-projection baseline; R1 the training-free metric-aware bridge; R2 the learned
quantiser, trained against the FLPSI accept objective on the 25,088-dimensional pre-pooling features
(not the 16-D embedding), on a split disjoint from both the CNN's training split and the evaluation
set, with early stopping on a held-out validation split. All EER intervals are 95\% bootstrap over
\emph{identities} (1,000 resamples), since pairs sharing a finger are not independent. Every number is
measured against the real frozen CNN and, where marked, the real \texttt{flash-psi} backend
(RTX 4050, CNN forward pass 0.80\,ms/image).

On Altered-Easy, R2 reaches $0.17\%$ EER $[0.06,0.26]$, roughly $5\times$ below R1 ($0.87\%$) and
$11\times$ below Super-Bit ($1.89\%$), all three intervals non-overlapping, and the ordering holds
across Medium and Hard. The mechanism is the impostor $\sigma$: $20.11 \to 14.60 \to 6.72$ against the
uniform ideal $5.66$, with the $H{=}0$ collision count dropping from 2 to 0. The jump from R1 to R2 is
about \emph{what} is coded: Super-Bit and R1 both quantise the 16-dimensional embedding, capping any
binariser on it at ${\sim}16$ independent bits, while R2 codes the 25,088-dimensional pre-pooling
feature map directly, where far more independent directions survive. SOCOFing's \texttt{Altered-*}
probes are synthetic same-capture alterations, optimistic relative to independent recaptures.

\subsection{The coupling demonstrated: communication}
\label{sec:eval-comms}

Online FLPSI communication scales with $T$: each sub-sample is a garbled-circuit/VOLE exchange over
the $N$-record database, so halving $T$ at fixed accuracy roughly halves protocol bytes.
Proposition~\ref{prop:tmin} gives the minimal $T$ for a target $(\mathrm{FAR},\mathrm{FRR})$ as a
function of $q_g, q_i$, which are set by the code's Hamming distributions, so the same $\sigma$ that
lowers EER also lowers the $T$ the decision rule needs, which lowers bytes. One coupling constant, two
payoffs.

At matched targets (TAR $\geq95\%$, per-record FAR $\leq10^{-2}$, $N=5{,}000$, real \texttt{flash-psi}
binary), R1 reaches its communication-minimal point at $(19,3,34)$ for $4317$\,KB (TAR $95.5\%$, FAR
$9.4\times10^{-3}$) and R2 at $(6,3,18)$ for $2377$\,KB (TAR $96.2\%$, FAR $7.9\times10^{-3}$), a
$\mathbf{-45\%}$ reduction; neither code is feasible at the other's $(w,t)$, so the code changes which
operating points exist, not just how good they are. Separately, the untuned Super-Bit $T=64$ point
(no feasible matched point) costs $7953$\,KB, so R2 is $\sim3.3\times$ smaller than that fixed
reference. The full swept Pareto frontier is left to the extended version.

\subsection{Real-crypto validation}

We validate the closed-form accept model against the real \texttt{flash-psi} binary (masked-OPRF +
garbled circuit + VOLE + Shamir sharing) at the R2 point $(6,3,18)$, 300 queries $\times$ 300 records:
real TAR $97.0\%$ and real per-record FAR $8.02\times10^{-3}$ against the predicted $8.12\times10^{-3}$,
a $1.2\%$ relative agreement. The same holds for Super-Bit and R1 at $(14,2,64)$ (real $4.42\times
10^{-2}$ vs.\ predicted $4.45\times10^{-2}$; real $1.05\times10^{-1}$ vs.\ predicted $1.05\times10^{-1}$),
so the surrogate used in \S\ref{sec:eval-ladder}--\ref{sec:eval-comms} is not a model artifact.

Two measured conclusions. The $\sigma$-collapse from R1 to R2 is universal, on every corpus including
those where R2 loses on EER (e.g.\ PolyU-contact $13.0\to7.27$, FVC2002 Db4\_a $20.2\to6.90$), so the
communication benefit travels even where the accuracy benefit does not. And the EER gain tracks the
\emph{quantisation gap} between the float representation and the 16-D embedding, as
Theorem~\ref{thm:relaxation} predicts: on SOCOFing the 25,088-D conv features are far richer than the
16-D bottleneck and R2 closes most of the gap, whereas on PolyU-contact and FVC2002 Db4\_a they are not
richer, there is no gap, and R2 is a wash or a loss. Gap-dependence confirms the mechanism. An
off-the-shelf DeepPrint-512~\cite{EngelsmaCaoJain19} extractor without fine-tuning transfers near chance
here (float EER 23.9--24.1\% on PolyU, 32.2--42.9\% on FVC2002), so the binding constraint is domain
adaptation, not coder design. Four code-side variants measured negative and are not pursued: joint
end-to-end CNN fine-tuning, a BCE bit-balance loss (inflates $\sigma$ $14.6\to18.6$), fragile-bit
pruning, and CLAHE/ROI preprocessing; cross-sensor transfer is near chance, scoping the work to
same-sensor deployment.

\subsection{Comparison with Blind-Touch}

Against the published Blind-Touch result~\cite{ChoiWooKim24} we win SOCOFing accuracy by roughly
$4\times$ (0.17\% vs.\ 0.7\%) and on deployment simplicity: a two-party protocol with label-only
disclosure, a 16\,B server-side template against 1.67\,KB, and no 117\,MB Galois key or 3-server HE
cluster. We lose on raw communication, $2377$\,KB against Blind-Touch's $856$\,KB of compressed-CKKS
traffic; our $-45\%$ narrows our own gap from roughly $9\times$ (at the untuned Super-Bit point) to
roughly $2.8\times$ but does not close it, and we do not claim otherwise. PolyU is not a controlled
comparison: Blind-Touch reports $2.5\%$ there with a corpus-specific extractor and ridge-alignment
pipeline, whereas our same-sensor PolyU EER (13.17--21.38\%) uses the SOCOFing-trained design with only
fine-tuning.

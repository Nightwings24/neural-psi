\section{Implementation and Evaluation}
\label{sec:eval}

\providecommand{\todo}[1]{\textbf{[TODO: #1]}}

This section makes the central claim of the paper concrete: the impostor Hamming
distribution of the binary code is the single object that drives \emph{both} accuracy
(EER, collision floor) and communication (the sub-sample count $T$, and hence protocol
bytes). We first diagnose the baseline code (Super-Bit) as the bottleneck
(\S\ref{sec:eval-ladder}), then show that fixing the code --- first with a training-free
metric-aware map (R1, ortho-thermometer, \S\ref{sec:ortho-thermo}), then with a learned,
quantisation-aware head (R2, feature-head, \S\ref{sec:featurehead}) --- improves accuracy
\emph{and} shrinks the communication needed to hit the same operating point
(\S\ref{sec:eval-comms}), on the same frozen CNN and the same 128-bit FLPSI backend
throughout.

\subsection{Setup}

\paragraph{Corpora and splits.} The extractor is a frozen Blind-Touch~\cite{ChoiWooKim24}
Siamese CNN trained on SOCOFing~\cite{Shehu18} (6,000 fingerprints, 600 subjects $\times$ 10
fingers), with an identity-disjoint split: 4,800 training images and 1,200 held-out
identities appearing in no training, calibration, or head-fitting step. Enrolment uses the
\texttt{Real} captures; probes use \texttt{Altered-\{Easy,Medium,Hard\}}. Generality is
checked on two further corpora, held out from all code training: PolyU (contact and
contactless, finger-disjoint splits) and FVC2002 (Db1--Db4, A-sets).

\paragraph{Protocol.} The 128-bit code feeds the FLPSI accept rule of
\S\ref{sec:accept-model}: a mask selects $w$ of 128 positions, a query and a record are
declared a sub-match iff they agree on all $w$ positions (probability
$q(H) = \binom{128-H}{w}/\binom{128}{w}$ as a function of Hamming distance $H$), and the
overall record accepts iff at least $t$ of $T$ independent sub-samples match
(Eqs.~\ref{eq:accept}--\ref{eq:rates}). We do not restate that derivation here.

\paragraph{Codes compared.} \emph{Super-Bit} is the original block-orthonormal random
projection baseline. \emph{R1 (ortho-thermometer)} is the training-free, metric-aware
bridge of \S\ref{sec:ortho-thermo}, which repairs the angular/Euclidean metric mismatch
identified as the root cause of Super-Bit's excess impostor variance. \emph{R2
(feature-head)} is the learned, quantisation-aware binariser of \S\ref{sec:featurehead},
trained end-to-end against the FLPSI accept objective on the 25,088-dimensional
pre-pooling convolutional features (not the 16-D embedding), on an identity split disjoint
from both the CNN's training split and the 1,200-identity held-out evaluation set, with
early stopping on a further held-out validation split --- i.e., no test-set peeking. This
is a difference in kind from R1: R1 is a fixed, training-free public map, R2 is a trained
quantiser.

\paragraph{Confidence intervals and provenance.} All EER intervals are 95\% bootstrap over
\emph{identities} (1,000 resamples), not pairs --- pairs sharing a finger are not
independent, and pair-level resampling understates the interval. Every number below is
measured against the real, frozen CNN and, where marked, the real \texttt{flash-psi}
backend (not a closed-form surrogate); values without a committed machine-readable
artifact were regenerated for this version. Hardware: RTX 4050 (6\,GB);
CNN forward pass 0.80\,ms/image (GPU).

Table~\ref{tab:acc} is the headline: on Altered-Easy, R2 reaches $0.17\%$ EER
$[0.06,0.26]$, roughly $5\times$ below R1 ($0.87\%$) and roughly $11\times$ below the
Super-Bit baseline ($1.89\%$), with all three intervals non-overlapping. The same ordering
holds, monotonically, across Medium and Hard. The mechanism is the impostor Hamming
standard deviation $\sigma$: it drops $20.11 \to 14.60 \to 6.72$ against a uniform-code
ideal of $5.66$ (Eq.~\ref{eq:vardecomp}), and the $H{=}0$ collision count on the
probe$\times$gallery set drops from 2 (Super-Bit; Poisson-rate $1.39\times10^{-6}$, 95\% CI
$[1.68\times10^{-7}, 5.02\times10^{-6}]$) to 0 for both R1 and R2.

\paragraph{Effective bits.} The Daugman degrees-of-freedom estimate
$m_{\mathrm{eff}} = d^2/(4\sigma^2)$ (Eq.~\ref{eq:meff}, $d=128$) rises from $\sim$10
(Super-Bit; participation-ratio estimate 9.84, consistent) to $\sim$14--18 for R1
(participation-ratio 13.6, Daugman 17.9) to \textbf{90.7} for R2 (participation-ratio
84.6), all measured directly from the impostor distributions
(\texttt{featurehead-effective-bits.json} for R2). The jump from R1 to R2 is the
signature of \emph{what} is coded, not merely \emph{how}: Super-Bit and R1 both quantise
the 16-dimensional post-pooling embedding, so no binariser applied to it can exceed
${\sim}16$ independent bits regardless of how well it is conditioned; R2 escapes that
ceiling because it codes the 25,088-dimensional pre-pooling convolutional feature map
directly, where far more than 16 independent directions of discriminative variance
survive.

\paragraph{Caveats.} SOCOFing's \texttt{Altered-*} probes are synthetic, same-capture
alterations of the \texttt{Real} images, which is optimistic relative to genuinely
independent recaptures; R2 is a trained quantiser evaluated on an identity-disjoint split
with early stopping on a held-out validation split, which is the correct protocol but a
different threat model from R1's training-free public map.

\subsection{The coupling demonstrated: communication}
\label{sec:eval-comms}

Online FLPSI communication scales with the sub-sample count $T$: each of the $T$
sub-samples is a garbled-circuit/VOLE exchange over the $N$-record database, so halving
$T$ at fixed accuracy roughly halves protocol bytes. Proposition~\ref{prop:tmin} gives the
minimal $T$ needed to hit a target $(\mathrm{FAR}, \mathrm{FRR})$ as a function of the
per-sub-sample genuine/impostor accept probabilities $q_g, q_i$, which are themselves set
by the code's Hamming distributions --- so the \emph{same} $\sigma$ that lowers EER in
Table~\ref{tab:acc} also lowers the $T$ needed for the deployed decision rule, which lowers
bytes. This is the paper's cross-layer punchline: one coupling constant, two payoffs.

Two conclusions, both measured. First, the $\sigma$-collapse from R1 to R2 is universal:
it occurs on every corpus, including the two where R2 does not win on EER, so the
communication benefit of \S\ref{sec:eval-comms} is expected to travel even where the
accuracy benefit does not. Second, the EER gain tracks the \emph{quantisation gap} between
the floating-point representation and the 16-D embedding, exactly as
Theorem~\ref{thm:relaxation} predicts: on SOCOFing the 25,088-D conv features are far
richer than the 16-D bottleneck ($0.16\%$ vs.\ $0.33\%$ float ceilings), and R2 closes
most of that gap; on PolyU-contact and FVC-Db4\_a the conv features are \emph{not} richer
than the 16-D output (float-conv EER exceeds float-16D EER), there is no gap to close, and
R2 is a wash or a loss. We read gap-dependence as \emph{confirming} the theory, not as a
weakness: the method improves accuracy only where the representation it draws on carries
information the 16-D bottleneck discarded, and predicts correctly when it will not help.

As a further check that this is a genuine representation-richness effect rather than an
artifact of our particular CNN, an off-the-shelf DeepPrint-512~\cite{EngelsmaCaoJain19}
extractor, with no fingerprint-specific fine-tuning, transfers near-chance to these same
corpora (float EER 23.9--24.1\% on PolyU, 32.2--42.9\% on FVC2002) --- a stronger
off-the-shelf backbone cannot manufacture a quantisation gap that is not there; the binding
constraint is domain adaptation, not coder design.

\subsection{Measured negatives}

Consistent with a positive result built on a documented failure search rather than a
single lucky configuration, the following all measured negative and are reported for
completeness. (i) End-to-end fine-tuning of the CNN jointly with the code objective
diverges: perturbing the already-converged embedding destroys discriminability faster than
the code objective can repair it. (ii) A BCE-based bit-balance loss \emph{inflates}
$\sigma$ ($14.6 \to 18.6$) and worsens EER, the opposite of the intended effect --- not
every balance objective helps; ours (\S\ref{sec:featurehead}) was selected because it
does. (iii) Fragile-bit pruning/reweighting gives no gain: EER is monotonically worse as
bits are dropped ($128\to0.87\%$, $96\to1.57\%$, $64\to2.23\%$), so the extra bits are
carrying real signal, not noise, and should not be pruned. (iv) Cross-sensor transfer
(contact$\leftrightarrow$contactless) is near chance even when the extractor is trained on
PolyU directly (float EER $48.7\%$), which scopes this work to same-sensor deployment.
(v) A CLAHE-plus-ROI preprocessing attempt to close the PolyU domain gap made the floating
point EER worse, not better ($10.87\%\to15.07\%$), consistent with (iv): the gap is in the
sensor domain, not preprocessing.

We win SOCOFing accuracy by roughly $4\times$ (0.17\% vs.\ 0.7\%) and deployment
simplicity: a two-party protocol with label-only disclosure, 16\,B server-side storage
against 1.67\,KB, and no 117\,MB Galois key or HE cluster. We lose on raw communication:
2,377\,KB against Blind-Touch's 856\,KB of compressed-CKKS traffic. Our $-45\%$
improvement (\S\ref{sec:eval-comms}) narrows our own gap to Blind-Touch from roughly
$9\times$ (at the original untuned Super-Bit point) to roughly $2.8\times$, but does not
close it; compressed homomorphic ciphertext is still more communication-efficient per
query than our garbled-circuit sub-sampling scheme, and we do not claim otherwise. PolyU
is not a controlled comparison, since Blind-Touch there uses a corpus-specific extractor
and alignment pipeline that our design does not attempt to match.

\paragraph{HE-based private biometrics.} Blind-Touch~\cite{ChoiWooKim24} is our baseline and the
source of the extractor architecture: a CKKS matcher over encrypted float embeddings, run on a
3-server cluster, at 117\,MB key material and 856\,KB per query. Earlier work established the
Siamese-under-HE pattern~\cite{KimOhKim20,YangEtAl20}. Blind-Match~\cite{BlindMatch24} is a
\emph{dangerous neighbour}: it attains strong $1{:}N$ accuracy under CKKS with batched cosine
matching, reporting 99.55\% rank-1 on PolyU. Blind-Match attains this while keeping the HE trust
and cost model --- large key material, encrypted-inference servers, and a similarity-score
disclosure. We differ on three axes: (i) a lighter two-party protocol with no homomorphic layer;
(ii) a label-only disclosure model, in which the client learns an identity on a match and nothing
on a miss; and (iii) a \emph{matcher-agnostic design objective} --- our contribution is not a
better HE matcher but a code co-designed with the matcher's own communication functional,
orthogonal to which matcher is used. A further anti-example sharpens the same point: DeepPrint's
200-byte fixed-length representation~\cite{EngelsmaCaoJain19} is exactly the kind of compact float
template whose $1{:}N$ matching under HE motivates leaving HE in the first place --- the per-query
communication and key material of encrypting even a compact template at scale are precisely the
costs Blind-Touch and Blind-Match pay.

\paragraph{Fuzzy and threshold PSI (the star primitive's lineage).} Uzun et
al.~\cite{UzunEtAl21} is the correct primary citation for the FLPSI functionality and its
sub-sample/threshold accept model --- fuzzy \emph{labelled} PSI for private real-time biometric
search. Bui and Cong~\cite{BuiCong25} give the efficient vector ring-OLE backend we use for the
concrete crypto realisation, built on the VOLE-PSI
line~\cite{RindalSchoppmann21,RaghuramanRindal22} and silent OT/PCG~\cite{BoyleEtAl19}; we use it
as the backend, not as the source of the functionality. A separate line of work achieves exact
fuzzy matching in low-dimensional metric spaces --- van Baarsen and
Pu~\cite{vanBaarsenPu24}, Gao et al.~\cite{GaoEtAl24}, Chakraborti et
al.~\cite{ChakrabortiEtAl23}, and Garimella et al.~\cite{GarimellaEtAl23}. These protocols achieve
exact fuzzy matching in low-dimensional $L_p$ balls, but their cost is exponential in the
dimension $d$; for $d=128$ Hamming they are impractical, which is exactly why the
sub-sample/threshold route of Uzun and Bui--Cong --- and therefore the impostor-Hamming design
objective it induces --- is the right target for high-dimensional biometric codes. None of this
line addresses where the bit-strings come from; all assume the input is already a code under
Hamming distance. Our contribution supplies that input from a learned extractor and, more
importantly, identifies the matcher's own communication functional as the objective the code
should be trained to.

\paragraph{Learning-to-hash and deep supervised hashing (the actuator's lineage).} SimHash~\cite{Charikar02},
Super-Bit LSH~\cite{JiEtAl12} and ITQ~\cite{GongLazebnik11} are the classical bridges from real
vectors to binary codes. A substantial line of deep supervised hashing pursues balanced,
low-quantisation-error codes for retrieval accuracy: GreedyHash~\cite{SuEtAl18},
HashNet~\cite{CaoEtAl17}, central similarity quantisation~\cite{YuanEtAl20}, one-loss
quantisation~\cite{DoanYangLi22}, bit-entropy maximisation~\cite{LiVanGemert21}, and the maximal
coding-rate-reduction (MCR\textsuperscript{2}) principle~\cite{YuEtAl20}, which supply the
balance and decorrelation objectives our feature head borrows. We claim no new hashing mechanism.
What is new is the \emph{reason} to want such a code here: for a private matcher, the same
balance-and-decorrelation objective that this literature developed for retrieval accuracy is,
under FLPSI's accept rule, the protocol's own \emph{communication} functional --- so an
off-the-shelf hashing loss, pointed at the impostor-Hamming distribution instead of at retrieval
recall, becomes a cryptographic-cost optimiser. No prior hashing work optimises the code for a
private matcher's communication.

\paragraph{Biometric template protection and ISO/IEC 24745.} Fuzzy commitment~\cite{JuelsWattenberg99},
fuzzy vaults~\cite{JuelsSudan02}, fuzzy extractors~\cite{DodisEtAl04} and their reusable
variant~\cite{CanettiEtAl16} form the key-from-noisy-data line; cancelable
biometrics~\cite{RathaEtAl01}, BioHashing~\cite{TeohEtAl04} and index-of-max
hashing~\cite{JinEtAl18,DongJinTeoh19}, the latter with a known cryptanalysis~\cite{GhammamEtAl20},
form the cancelable-transform line. These schemes target exactly the ISO/IEC 24745 properties our
stored code must satisfy; we adopt a per-enrolment XOR mask for unlinkability and renewability
(\S\ref{sec:security}), which preserves Hamming distance exactly and so costs nothing in accuracy,
and we are explicit that it does not deliver irreversibility on its own --- a construction from
this line would need to be layered on. Gomez-Barrero and Galbally's survey of inverse
biometrics~\cite{GomezBarreroGalbally20} is the reconstruction threat that motivates taking
irreversibility seriously rather than treating it as a formality. Daugman's degrees-of-freedom
analysis~\cite{Daugman03} is the foundation of our effective-bits argument: a biometric code
carries a finite number of independent degrees of freedom, its impostor comparisons follow a
binomial law, and the variance of that law is exactly what our design objective (\S\ref{sec:design})
targets.

\paragraph{Fingerprint representation.} DeepPrint~\cite{EngelsmaCaoJain19} gives the fixed-length
neural representation that makes a binariser meaningful in the first place; Rohwedder et
al.~\cite{Rohwedder23} provide a public reimplementation benchmarked across embedding sizes and
sensor types, which we use as the backbone for a cross-corpus generality study; Pan et
al.~\cite{PanEtAl24} give recent state-of-the-art context with a dense minutia descriptor. The
cross-corpus study shows the extractor, not the code, is the bottleneck on harder corpora ---
which sharpens rather than weakens our central claim, since the communication benefit of a
co-designed code is code-side and, in our measurements, universal, while the accuracy benefit is
tied to how much larger the float embedding's discriminative power is than any fixed-length code
of it.

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{HE-based private biometrics.} Blind-Touch~\cite{ChoiWooKim24} is our baseline and the source
of the extractor architecture: a CKKS matcher over encrypted float embeddings on a 3-server cluster, at
117\,MB key material and 856\,KB per query, following the Siamese-under-HE
pattern~\cite{KimOhKim20}. Blind-Match~\cite{BlindMatch24} attains strong $1{:}N$ accuracy
under CKKS with batched cosine matching (99.55\% rank-1 on PolyU) while keeping the HE trust and cost
model. We differ on three axes: a two-party protocol with no homomorphic layer; a label-only
disclosure model; and a matcher-agnostic design objective, a code co-designed with the matcher's own
communication functional rather than a better HE matcher. DeepPrint's 200-byte
representation~\cite{EngelsmaCaoJain19} is the kind of compact float template whose $1{:}N$ matching
under HE motivates leaving HE in the first place.

\paragraph{Fuzzy and threshold PSI.} Uzun et al.~\cite{UzunEtAl21} is the primary citation for the FLPSI
functionality and its sub-sample/threshold accept model; Bui and Cong~\cite{BuiCong25} give the
efficient vector ring-OLE backend we use, built on the VOLE-PSI
line~\cite{RindalSchoppmann21} and silent OT/PCG~\cite{BoyleEtAl19}. A separate line
achieves exact fuzzy matching in low-dimensional metric
spaces~\cite{vanBaarsenPu24}, but at cost exponential in
$d$, so for $d=128$ Hamming the sub-sample/threshold route, and the impostor-Hamming objective it
induces, is the right target. None of this line addresses where the bit-strings come from; we supply
that input from a learned extractor and identify the matcher's own communication functional as the
objective the code should be trained to.

\paragraph{Learning-to-hash.} SimHash~\cite{Charikar02} and Super-Bit LSH~\cite{JiEtAl12} are the
classical bridges, and a line of deep supervised hashing pursues balanced, low-quantisation-error codes
for retrieval, which supply the balance and decorrelation objectives our feature head borrows. We claim no new hashing mechanism. What
is new is the \emph{reason} to want such a code here: under FLPSI's accept rule the
balance-and-decorrelation objective this literature developed for retrieval accuracy is the protocol's
own \emph{communication} functional, so an off-the-shelf hashing loss pointed at the impostor-Hamming
distribution becomes a cryptographic-cost optimiser. No prior hashing work optimises the code for a
private matcher's communication.

\paragraph{Template protection and fingerprint representation.} Fuzzy
commitment~\cite{JuelsWattenberg99}, cancelable biometrics, and index-of-max
hashing~\cite{JinEtAl18}, the latter with a known cryptanalysis~\cite{GhammamEtAl20}, target the
ISO/IEC 24745 properties our stored code must satisfy; we adopt a per-enrolment XOR mask
(\S\ref{sec:security}), explicit that it does not deliver irreversibility on its own. Gomez-Barrero and
Galbally~\cite{GomezBarreroGalbally20} is the reconstruction threat that motivates taking
irreversibility seriously, and Daugman's degrees-of-freedom analysis~\cite{Daugman03} founds our
effective-bits argument. DeepPrint~\cite{EngelsmaCaoJain19} gives the fixed-length representation that
makes a binariser meaningful; the cross-corpus study shows the extractor, not the code, is the
bottleneck on harder corpora, which sharpens our claim since the communication benefit is code-side and
universal while the accuracy benefit tracks the embedding's quantisation gap.

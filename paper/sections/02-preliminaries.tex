\subsection{Homomorphic encryption, and why we leave it}

CKKS~\cite{CKKS17} supports approximate arithmetic on encrypted vectors of reals, packing many
slots into one ciphertext; rotating between slots, which any inner product needs, requires Galois
keys that dominate key material. Blind-Touch~\cite{ChoiWooKim24} applies this to fingerprint
matching, giving strong confidentiality at the cost of a 117\,MB Galois key per client, 856\,KB
per query ciphertext, and a database organised around a 512-users-per-ciphertext packing limit.

\subsection{Siamese feature extraction}

A Siamese network~\cite{ChoiWooKim24,KimOhKim20,YangEtAl20} maps two images through a shared
convolutional trunk trained so that same-identity pairs land close. We reuse this unchanged: the
trunk produces a real-valued embedding $\mathbf{e}$, trained with pairwise binary cross-entropy on
identity-disjoint splits. The architecture is not a contribution here; what matters is only that
the output is a real vector whose distance under the extractor's training metric tracks identity,
and that it must be turned into a fixed-length binary code before a fuzzy-PSI matcher can consume
it.

\subsection{Fuzzy-labelled PSI: functionality and accept model}
\label{sec:flpsi-prelim}

Fuzzy-labelled PSI (FLPSI) was introduced by Uzun et al.\ for private real-time biometric
search~\cite{UzunEtAl21}: it lets a client holding $\xt \in \{0,1\}^{d}$ learn the label
$\ell_i$ of any server record $\yt_i$ within a Hamming ball of the query, while the server learns
nothing beyond whether a match occurred. We take this functionality and its accept model from
Uzun et al.; the concrete efficient backend we use for the crypto realisation is Bui and Cong's
vector ring-OLE construction~\cite{BuiCong25}, itself built on the VOLE-PSI
line~\cite{RindalSchoppmann21,RaghuramanRindal22} and silent OT/PCG~\cite{BoyleEtAl19}. We
restate the accept model as a black box, only as far as our design objective in
\S\ref{sec:design} depends on it.

A \textbf{masked OPRF} lets the two parties evaluate a keyed PRF on sub-sampled positions of
their inputs, so that equality of the PRF images certifies equality on those positions without
revealing the underlying bits. The parties agree on $T$ public masks $m_j \in \{0,1\}^{d}$ of
weight $w$; sub-sample $j$ of a code is its restriction to the positions selected by $m_j$.

A \textbf{threshold labelled PSI} then converts collisions into a label. The server splits each
$\ell_i$ into Shamir shares~\cite{Shamir79}, attaches one share per mask, and delivers them
through a polynomial encoding evaluated obliviously via the OLE/VOLE backend above. A client
whose code collides on at least $t$ masks recovers enough shares to reconstruct $\ell_i$; a
client with fewer collisions learns nothing about it.

\paragraph{The accept-probability functional.} Two codes at Hamming distance $H$ collide on a
given mask, drawn as a uniformly random $w$-subset of the $d$ positions, with exact (hypergeometric)
probability
\[
q(H) \;=\; \binom{d-H}{w}\Big/\binom{d}{w} \;\approx\; \left(1 - \frac{H}{d}\right)^{w}
\qquad (w \ll d).
\]
The $T$ sub-samples are drawn independently, so the number of colliding masks $K$ satisfies
$K \mid H \sim \mathrm{Binomial}(T, q(H))$, and the client accepts iff at least $t$ masks
collide:
\[
\Pr[\text{accept} \mid H] \;=\; \Pr[K \ge t] \;=\; \sum_{k=t}^{T} \binom{T}{k} q(H)^{k}\,
\bigl(1-q(H)\bigr)^{T-k}.
\]
Writing $P_{\mathrm{gen}}$ and $P_{\mathrm{imp}}$ for the distribution of $H$ over genuine and
impostor pairs respectively, the aggregate error rates are exact functionals of these two
histograms:
\[
\mathrm{FAR} = \sum_{H} P_{\mathrm{imp}}(H)\,\Pr[\text{accept}\mid H], \qquad
\mathrm{FRR} = 1 - \sum_{H} P_{\mathrm{gen}}(H)\,\Pr[\text{accept}\mid H].
\]
This is a \emph{label-only} disclosure model throughout: a match reveals $\ell_i$ and nothing
else; a non-match reveals nothing. Communication is linear in $N$, scaling with $T$, and the only
key material is a PRF key held by the server; there is no packing ceiling and no Galois key.
\S\ref{sec:design} develops the design consequence of this accept model --- that FAR, FRR and $T$
are all governed by one statistic of $P_{\mathrm{imp}}$ --- into the coupling theorem that is this
paper's core contribution.

\subsection{Binary codes from real vectors}

SimHash~\cite{Charikar02} maps a vector to the signs of random hyperplane projections; for unit
vectors at angle $\vartheta$ a random hyperplane separates them with probability $\vartheta/\pi$,
so normalised Hamming distance estimates $\vartheta/\pi$ without bias. \textbf{Super-Bit
LSH}~\cite{JiEtAl12} orthonormalises the projections in blocks equal to the input dimension,
which leaves the estimator unbiased while strictly reducing its variance at the acute angles that
separate genuine from impostor pairs; at block size one it degenerates to SimHash.
\textbf{ITQ}~\cite{GongLazebnik11} instead learns a rotation minimising quantisation loss, and is
data-dependent. These are the classical, matcher-agnostic bridges; \S\ref{sec:design} shows why,
under FLPSI's accept model, the choice of bridge is not matcher-agnostic in effect, and derives
the objective a bridge should be designed against instead.

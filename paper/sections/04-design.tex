\section{Design and the Coupling Theorem}
\label{sec:design}

\subsection{System design}
\label{sec:design-overview}

NeuralPSI is a two-party protocol between a client and a server. A Siamese CNN feature
extractor runs entirely on the client, in plaintext, over the raw capture; a public,
fixed binariser turns its embedding into a $d$-bit code $\xt \in \{0,1\}^d$ ($d = 128$);
and the two parties run fuzzy-labelled private set intersection (FLPSI) --- random
$w$-subset sub-sampling, a count-threshold accept rule, label-only output --- to decide
whether $\xt$ matches an enrolled code $\yt_i$ and, if so, to reveal only the associated
label $\ell_i$. The server never learns $\xt$, any sub-sample of it, or the Hamming
distance to non-matching records; it learns a label or nothing. This functionality and
its accept model are due to \cite{UzunEtAl21}; we build a private, label-revealing
matcher on top of it and, in \S\ref{sec:security}, prove what a corrupt client and a
corrupt server each learn. The threat model, the two corruption cases, and the
credentialing step that turns a private lookup into an authentication are given in
\S\ref{sec:threat} and \S\ref{sec:security}; this section is about what happens
\emph{before} the protocol runs --- how the client's code is chosen --- and why that
choice is not incidental to the protocol's accuracy or its communication cost, but
governs both through a single scalar.

The central design question is which binariser to use. \S\ref{sec:binarisers} gives two:
a training-free, metric-aware ortho-thermometer code, and a quantisation-aware learned
feature-head. Both are instances of the same target established below, and the
remainder of this section proves why that target is the right one.

\subsection{Notation and setup}
\label{sec:coupling-setup}

The server holds enrolled codes $\yt_1,\dots,\yt_N \in \{0,1\}^d$; the client holds a
probe $\xt$. For a fixed pair write $H = H(\xt,\yt) = \lVert \xt \oplus \yt\rVert_1 \in
\{0,\dots,d\}$ for their Hamming distance, and $D_i = \mathbf{1}[\xt_i \neq \yt_i]$ for
the per-position difference indicator, so $H = \sum_{i=1}^d D_i$. FLPSI draws $T$
independent uniformly random $w$-subsets $S_1,\dots,S_T \subseteq [d]$ and accepts iff at
least $t$ of them collide, i.e.\ $\xt$ and $\yt$ agree on every position of $S_k$ for at
least $t$ values of $k$.

\paragraph{Per-trial match probability.} A single trial collides iff $\xt,\yt$ agree on
all of a uniformly random $w$-subset:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:qh}
q(H) \;=\; \frac{\binom{d-H}{w}}{\binom{d}{w}} \;\approx\; \Bigl(1-\frac{H}{d}\Bigr)^{w}
\end{equation}
(hypergeometric exactly; the sampling-with-replacement approximation is tight for $w \ll
d$). Writing $K$ for the number of colliding trials out of $T$, $K \mid H \sim
\mathrm{Binomial}(T, q(H))$, so
\begin{equation}
\Pr[\text{accept}\mid H] \;=\; \Pr[K \ge t] \;=\; \sum_{k=t}^{T} \binom{T}{k} q(H)^k
\bigl(1-q(H)\bigr)^{T-k}.
\end{equation}
Let $P_{\mathrm{gen}}$, $P_{\mathrm{imp}}$ denote the distribution of $H$ over genuine and
impostor pairs respectively. The aggregate error rates are exact functionals of these two
histograms:
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{FAR} = \sum_H P_{\mathrm{imp}}(H)\,\Pr[\text{accept}\mid H], \qquad
\mathrm{FRR} = 1 - \sum_H P_{\mathrm{gen}}(H)\,\Pr[\text{accept}\mid H].
\end{equation}
We write $q_g = \mathbb{E}_{H \sim P_{\mathrm{gen}}}[q(H)]$ and $q_i =
\mathbb{E}_{H\sim P_{\mathrm{imp}}}[q(H)]$ for the aggregated per-trial genuine and
impostor accept masses.

Four assumptions underlie everything below, and each is validated against measurement
rather than merely posited.
\begin{description}
\item[(A1) Position exchangeability.] $\Pr[\text{accept}\mid H]$ depends on a pair only
  through $H$, not on \emph{which} positions differ, because $S_k$ is a uniformly random
  $w$-subset. This is exact by construction of the protocol.
\item[(A2) Conditional hypergeometric accept.] Given $H$, a sub-sample collides with
  probability $q(H)$ even though the underlying bits are dependent across positions.
  Measured $\hat q(H)/q(H)$ has median $1.002$ over $H \in [5,40]$ at $w=14$.
  Bit dependence is real but manifests in the
  \emph{distribution of $H$}, not in $\Pr[\text{accept}\mid H]$ --- which is what isolates
  the entire modelling problem to $P_{\mathrm{imp}}$ and $P_{\mathrm{gen}}$, and is why
  the coupling below is exact rather than heuristic at the level of the accept rule.
\item[(A3) Independence across trials.] The $T$ sub-samples are drawn independently, so
  $K \mid H \sim \mathrm{Binomial}(T, q(H))$ exactly.
\item[(A4) Communication model.] Online per-query communication scales linearly in $T$:
  the masked OPRF and the vector ring-OLE label encoding are each repeated per mask.
\end{description}

\subsection{The coupling analysis}
\label{sec:coupling}

We now show that a single scalar functional of the code --- the variance of the impostor
Hamming law --- governs simultaneously the achievable (FAR, FRR) operating point and the
number of sub-sampling rounds $T$ needed to reach it, and hence, by (A4), the online
communication cost. A code that minimizes this functional is therefore accuracy-optimal
and communication-optimal at once; this is the paper's central mechanism.

\begin{lemma}[Cost is a functional of the impostor-Hamming law]
\label{lem:jensen}
Let $g(h) = (1-h/d)^w$ on $h \in [0,d]$, $w \ge 2$. Then $g$ is convex and strictly
decreasing, and the effective per-trial impostor accept mass satisfies
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:jensen}
q_i \;=\; \mathbb{E}_{H \sim P_{\mathrm{imp}}}[g(H)] \;\ge\; g(\mathbb{E}[H]) \;=\;
\Bigl(1-\frac{\mathbb{E}[H]}{d}\Bigr)^{w}.
\end{equation}
Moreover the gap $q_i - g(\mathbb{E}[H])$ is monotone under mean-preserving spreads of
$P_{\mathrm{imp}}$ (the convex order), and to second order equals
$\tfrac12 g''(\mathbb{E}[H])\,\mathrm{Var}(H) + O(\text{third central moment})$. In
particular, at fixed mean $\mathbb{E}[H] = d/2$, larger impostor variance implies larger
$q_i$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
\emph{Monotonicity and convexity.} $g'(h) = -(w/d)(1-h/d)^{w-1} \le 0$ on $[0,d]$, so $g$
is decreasing; $g''(h) = \bigl(w(w-1)/d^2\bigr)(1-h/d)^{w-2} \ge 0$ for $w \ge 2$, so $g$
is convex (strictly on $[0,d)$).

\emph{Jensen.} Convexity gives $\mathbb{E}[g(H)] \ge g(\mathbb{E}[H])$, i.e.\
\eqref{eq:jensen}.

\emph{Monotone in the convex order.} If $H \le_{\mathrm{cx}} H'$ (equal means, $H'$ a
mean-preserving spread of $H$), then $\mathbb{E}[g(H)] \le \mathbb{E}[g(H')]$ for every
convex $g$, by definition of the convex order. Spreading $P_{\mathrm{imp}}$ at fixed mean
can therefore only increase $q_i$.

\emph{Second order.} Taylor-expanding around $\mu = \mathbb{E}[H]$,
$\mathbb{E}[g(H)] = g(\mu) + \tfrac12 g''(\mu)\mathrm{Var}(H) +
\tfrac16 g'''(\mu)\mathbb{E}[(H-\mu)^3] + \cdots$. Since $g'' \ge 0$, the variance term is
non-negative; at $\mu = d/2$, $g''(d/2) = \bigl(w(w-1)/d^2\bigr)(1/2)^{w-2} > 0$, so the
leading-order gap is strictly increasing in $\mathrm{Var}(H)$. \qed
\end{proof}

The rigorous statement is the convex-order one: a mean-preserving spread of
$P_{\mathrm{imp}}$ strictly increases $q_i$. Plain variance is the operational,
second-order surrogate for that statement, and it is what we track empirically; two laws
with equal mean and variance but different skew can still differ in $q_i$ through
$g'''$ and higher terms. The paper's central diagnostic fact --- an impostor law with
$\sigma = 20.11$ against the uniform ideal $\sigma = 5.66$ at the identical mean $64$ ---
is exactly such a mean-preserving spread, and Lemma~\ref{lem:jensen} is why it inflates
$q_i$, and (Proposition~\ref{prop:tmin} below) the required $T$.

\begin{proposition}[Neyman--Pearson optimality of the count threshold]
\label{prop:np}
Fix $(w,T)$ and let $K$ be the number of colliding trials for a pair whose per-trial
accept mass is $q \in \{q_i, q_g\}$, $q_g > q_i$. $K$ is a sufficient statistic for
deciding genuine versus impostor within the family $K \sim \mathrm{Binomial}(T,q)$, and
the likelihood ratio
\begin{equation}
\Lambda(k) \;=\; \frac{q_g^{\,k}(1-q_g)^{T-k}}{q_i^{\,k}(1-q_i)^{T-k}}
\;=\; \left(\frac{q_g(1-q_i)}{q_i(1-q_g)}\right)^{\!k} \left(\frac{1-q_g}{1-q_i}\right)^{\!T}
\end{equation}
is monotone increasing in $k$. By the Neyman--Pearson lemma with a monotone likelihood
ratio, the test ``accept iff $K \ge t$'' is uniformly most powerful at its size: among all
decision rules on $T$ trials at fixed $(w,T)$, thresholding $K$ minimizes FRR at any given
FAR.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
$q_g > q_i \Rightarrow q_g(1-q_i)/\bigl(q_i(1-q_g)\bigr) > 1$, so $\Lambda(k)$ is
increasing in $k$. The binomial family is exponential in $k$ with natural parameter
$\log[q/(1-q)]$, monotone in $q$; a monotone likelihood ratio implies the Neyman--Pearson
optimal test at any size is a threshold test on the sufficient statistic
\cite{CoverThomas06}. \qed
\end{proof}

Proposition~\ref{prop:np} is a positive statement about the protocol as given: FLPSI's
count-threshold accept rule is not an ad hoc heuristic but the optimal detector for the
sufficient statistic the protocol computes. It does not, by itself, say how many rounds
$T$ are required; that is next.

\begin{proposition}[Required $T$]
\label{prop:tmin}
Model $K \mid \text{impostor} \sim \mathcal{N}(Tq_i, Tq_i(1-q_i))$ and $K \mid
\text{genuine} \sim \mathcal{N}(Tq_g, Tq_g(1-q_g))$. Placing $t$ to hit $\mathrm{FAR} =
\alpha$ from the impostor (null) side and $\mathrm{FRR} = \beta$ from the genuine
(alternative) side requires
\begin{equation}
t = Tq_i + z_\alpha\sqrt{Tq_i(1-q_i)}, \qquad t = Tq_g - z_\beta\sqrt{Tq_g(1-q_g)},
\end{equation}
with $z_\alpha = \Phi^{-1}(1-\alpha)$, $z_\beta = \Phi^{-1}(1-\beta)$. Equating and
solving for $T$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:tmin-gauss}
T_{\min} \;\approx\; \frac{\Bigl[z_\alpha\sqrt{q_i(1-q_i)} + z_\beta\sqrt{q_g(1-q_g)}\Bigr]^2}
{(q_g - q_i)^2}.
\end{equation}
Equivalently, by the Chernoff--Stein lemma, fixing $\mathrm{FRR} \le \beta$ at a constant
makes the minimal attainable FAR decay as $\mathrm{FAR} \doteq \exp\bigl(-T\cdot
D(\mathrm{Bern}(q_g)\,\|\,\mathrm{Bern}(q_i))\bigr)$, so
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:tmin-stein}
T_{\min} \;\approx\; \frac{\ln(1/\mathrm{FAR})}{D(\mathrm{Bern}(q_g)\,\|\,\mathrm{Bern}(q_i))},
\qquad
D(\mathrm{Bern}(a)\,\|\,\mathrm{Bern}(b)) = a\ln\frac{a}{b} + (1-a)\ln\frac{1-a}{1-b}.
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The Gaussian form follows the usual two-sided threshold-placement argument for a
CLT approximation to a binomial count, matching $z_\alpha$ to the impostor/null spread
$q_i(1-q_i)$ (type-I control) and $z_\beta$ to the genuine/alternative spread
$q_g(1-q_g)$ (type-II control), then solving the two threshold equations for $T$. The
error-exponent form is the Chernoff--Stein lemma \cite{CoverThomas06}: with one error
probability held fixed, the other decays exponentially in $T$ at rate given by the
relative entropy between the alternative and the null, in that order, because Stein's
lemma fixes the type-II error and optimizes the type-I exponent under $H_1$. \qed
\end{proof}

Both forms make the same structural point: $T_{\min}$ scales as $1/(q_g-q_i)^2$ in the
Gaussian form and inversely with a KL divergence between $\mathrm{Bern}(q_g)$ and
$\mathrm{Bern}(q_i)$ in the exponential form, so shrinking $q_i$ --- which
Lemma~\ref{lem:jensen} ties directly to the impostor-Hamming variance --- simultaneously
widens the gap $q_g - q_i$ and grows the divergence, and is the dominant lever on the
number of rounds the protocol needs. (If instead FAR is held fixed and FRR is driven
down, the exponent is $D(\mathrm{Bern}(q_i)\,\|\,\mathrm{Bern}(q_g))$ rather than
\eqref{eq:tmin-stein}; KL divergence is asymmetric, and which error is held fixed
determines which direction applies. We report \eqref{eq:tmin-stein}, the statement
relevant to reaching a FAR target at a fixed true-accept rate.)

\paragraph{Finite-$T$ caveat.} Proposition~\ref{prop:tmin} is asymptotic --- a CLT
approximation and a large-deviation exponent. At the small $T$ actually deployed (e.g.\
$T=18$) we use the exact binomial tail $\Pr[K \ge t]$ for design, and validate the result
against the real cryptographic binary rather than the asymptotic formula alone: at
$(w,t,T) = (6,3,18)$ the feature-head code (\S\ref{sec:binarisers}) gives measured TAR
97.0\% and measured FAR $8.02\times10^{-3}$ ($0.80\%$) against a predicted $8.12\times10^{-3}$,
and earlier codes matched their binomial
predictions essentially exactly. We present the
asymptotics of Proposition~\ref{prop:tmin} as design guidance and the executed protocol
as ground truth (\S\ref{sec:eval}).

\subsubsection{The variance decomposition and the covariance floor}

Lemma~\ref{lem:jensen} and Proposition~\ref{prop:tmin} together reduce the entire design
problem to one target: minimize $\mathrm{Var}(H_{\mathrm{imp}})$ at a mean that keeps
$q_g$ usable. The next theorem shows this variance decomposes into a diagonal term with a
hard ceiling and an off-diagonal covariance term with no floor other than zero, which
identifies exactly what a good code must remove.

For an impostor pair, write $p_i = \Pr[D_i = 1] = \mathbb{E}[D_i]$ for the per-position
impostor flip probability, so $\mathbb{E}[H] = \sum_i p_i$ and $\mathrm{Var}(D_i) =
p_i(1-p_i)$.

\begin{theorem}[Variance decomposition and the covariance floor]
\label{thm:variance}
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:vardecomp}
\mathrm{Var}(H_{\mathrm{imp}}) \;=\; \sum_{i=1}^{d} p_i(1-p_i) \;+\; \sum_{i \neq j}
\mathrm{Cov}(D_i,D_j).
\end{equation}
The diagonal term is bounded, $p_i(1-p_i) \le 1/4$ with equality iff $p_i = 1/2$
(a balanced bit), so $\sum_i p_i(1-p_i) \le d/4$, attained exactly at full balance. The
uniform/max-entropy reference code has every $p_i = 1/2$ and every
$\mathrm{Cov}(D_i,D_j) = 0$, giving $H \sim \mathrm{Binomial}(d,1/2)$,
$\mathrm{Var} = d/4$, $\sigma = \sqrt{d}/2$ ($= 5.657$ at $d=128$). Consequently every
unit of impostor variance above the binomial floor $d/4$ is positive inter-bit
covariance: at full balance $\mathrm{Var}(H) - d/4 = \sum_{i\neq j}\mathrm{Cov}(D_i,D_j)$,
and if the bits are not fully balanced then $\sum_i p_i(1-p_i) < d/4$, so the covariance
surplus required to explain the observed variance is even larger.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Bilinearity of variance applied to the sum $H = \sum_i D_i$ of dependent Bernoullis gives
\eqref{eq:vardecomp} directly. The bound $p(1-p) \le 1/4$ is elementary, maximized at
$p=1/2$. The reference-code moments are those of $\mathrm{Binomial}(d,1/2)$. The floor
identity is \eqref{eq:vardecomp} rearranged. \qed
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[What to minimize]
\label{cor:target}
Among all impostor laws with mean $d/2$, $\mathrm{Var}(H_{\mathrm{imp}})$ --- and hence,
by Lemma~\ref{lem:jensen} (to second order, or exactly in the convex order), $q_i$; and
hence, by Proposition~\ref{prop:tmin}, $T$; and hence, by~(A4), online communication; and
hence the achievable $(\mathrm{FAR},\mathrm{FRR})$ operating point --- is minimized by the
\textbf{balanced and decorrelated} code, whose impostor law is $\mathrm{Binomial}(d,1/2)$
with $\sigma = 5.66$. Accuracy and communication are governed by the same scalar
functional of the code, so a single improvement to the code improves both. This is the
paper's central mechanism: the measured impostor $\sigma$ ladder $20.11 \to 14.60 \to
6.72$ tracks the equal-error-rate ladder $1.89\%\;[1.57,2.22] \to 0.87\%\;[0.62,0.99] \to
0.17\%\;[0.06,0.26]$ and the per-query communication at $N=5000$, $4317 \to 2377$~KB
($-45\%$ from ortho-thermometer to feature-head).
\end{corollary}

\subsubsection{The feature-head objective as a relaxation of Corollary~\ref{cor:target}}

Corollary~\ref{cor:target} specifies a population program: minimize the covariance
surplus $\sum_{i\neq j}\mathrm{Cov}(D_i,D_j)$ subject to $\mathbb{E}[D_i] = 1/2$ for all
$i$ (balance, hence $\mathbb{E}[H]=d/2$) and a small genuine Hamming distance
$\mathbb{E}_{\mathrm{gen}}[H]$ (so that $q_g$ stays large enough that the feasible set is
non-trivial). The learned feature-head (\S\ref{sec:featurehead}) is trained against a loss
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L} \;=\; \lambda_{\mathrm{gen}}\cdot\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{gen}} \;+\;
\lambda_{\mathrm{bal}}\cdot\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{bal}} \;+\;
\lambda_{\mathrm{dec}}\cdot\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{dec}} \;+\;
\lambda_{q}\cdot\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{STE}}
\end{equation}
with $(\lambda_{\mathrm{dec}},\lambda_{\mathrm{bal}},\lambda_q) = (30,5,0.1)$.

\begin{theorem}[The feature-head loss is a sampled relaxation of the population program]
\label{thm:relaxation}
Term by term, $\mathcal{L}$ is a differentiable, sample-average surrogate of the program
implied by Corollary~\ref{cor:target}: $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{bal}}$ pins
$\mathbb{E}[D_i]=1/2$, enforcing the balance constraint; $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{dec}}$
penalizes the off-diagonal empirical bit-covariance matrix, driving
$\sum_{i\neq j}\mathrm{Cov}(D_i,D_j)\to 0$, which is the objective itself;
$\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{gen}}$ keeps $\mathbb{E}_{\mathrm{gen}}[H]$ small, i.e.\ feasibility
and a usable $q_g$; and the straight-through estimator in $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{STE}}$
makes the otherwise non-differentiable sign binarisation trainable.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}[Correspondence, stated as tightly as is honest]
\emph{Exact at the level of the second-moment functional.} $\mathrm{Var}(H_{\mathrm{imp}})$
depends only on the first and second moments $\{p_i\}, \{\mathrm{Cov}(D_i,D_j)\}$
(Theorem~\ref{thm:variance}), a linear --- hence convex --- functional of the bit
second-moment matrix. Balancing ($p_i=1/2$) and zeroing the off-diagonal covariance
therefore attains the exact variance floor $d/4$ with no dependence on higher moments,
so the balance-plus-decorrelation objective is a \emph{tight} surrogate for the variance
objective, and the population program is convex in the bit second-moment matrix.

\emph{Heuristic step (flagged, not glossed over).} The map from network weights to
$\{p_i, \mathrm{Cov}(D_i,D_j)\}$ is non-convex, and the straight-through sign estimator is
a biased gradient surrogate for the true (zero-a.e.) gradient of the sign function. The
convexity claim above holds for the objective \emph{as a functional of the code's bit
distribution}, not for the optimization landscape over network weights; we do not claim,
and this theorem does not establish, global optimality of trained solutions.

\emph{Honest gap: a variance floor is not a law.} Zero linear covariance does not imply
independence, and matching the first two moments does not force the full impostor law to
$\mathrm{Binomial}(d,1/2)$; higher moments are uncontrolled. What
Corollary~\ref{cor:target} actually requires is only the variance, via the second-order
term of Lemma~\ref{lem:jensen}, and balance-plus-decorrelation pins that exactly; the
stronger statement ``the impostor law converges to $\mathrm{Binomial}(d,1/2)$'' is
moment-matching evidence, not a proved law-convergence result, and should be read as
``moves toward the uniform ideal'' --- consistent with the observed $\sigma \to 6.72
\approx 5.66$, not as an exact-law theorem.

\emph{Sample-average approximation.} The losses are computed as empirical covariances
over minibatches of impostor pairs, which are consistent estimators of the corresponding
population moments; the trained objective is thus the standard sample-average
approximation of the population program, with the usual SAA consistency guarantees and no
stronger claim than that. \qed
\end{proof}

Theorem~\ref{thm:relaxation} is deliberately not a claim that the feature-head ``beats''
the protocol. It co-designs the code \emph{for} the protocol's own optimal decision rule
(Proposition~\ref{prop:np}), pushing the impostor law toward the point at which the
protocol's sufficient statistic is maximally informative. There is also a real tension
the theorem does not resolve: tightening $\sigma$ can raise the \emph{genuine} Hamming
distance as a side effect, which lowers $q_g$; the
single-finger EER and communication improvements reported here are robust, but
multi-sample fusion is a tunable trade-off rather than an unconditional win.

\subsubsection{Effective bits and the collision floor}
\label{sec:effbits}

The variance functional also gives a rigorous degrees-of-freedom estimate. Modelling the
impostor Hamming \emph{fraction} $X = H/d$ as $\mathrm{Binomial}(m_{\mathrm{eff}},p)/m_{\mathrm{eff}}$
with per-comparison success $p = \mathbb{E}[H]/d$ and matching the variance of the
fraction, $\mathrm{Var}(X) = \sigma^2/d^2 = p(1-p)/m_{\mathrm{eff}}$, gives
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:meff}
m_{\mathrm{eff}} \;=\; \frac{p(1-p)\,d^2}{\sigma^2} \;=\; \frac{d^2}{4\sigma^2} \quad
\text{at balance } (p=1/2),
\end{equation}
the Daugman effective-bits estimator \cite{Daugman03}. As a sanity check, the ideal
uniform code has $\sigma = \sqrt{d}/2$, giving $m_{\mathrm{eff}} = d$. Table~\ref{tab:meff}
applies \eqref{eq:meff} to the three measured codes.

Recovering effective bits explains \emph{why} the design in \S\ref{sec:binarisers} works:
a 16-dimensional embedding bottleneck limits Super-Bit to roughly 10 effective bits of
128 nominal, near a structural ceiling; the wider convolutional feature representation
used by the feature-head lifts that ceiling toward $90.7$ (participation-ratio estimate
$84.6$). No dedicated fragile-bits measurement exists on the feature-head code; the
value above is derived from the measured $\sigma$, not from an independent fragile-bits
run.

\paragraph{An alternative, distribution-free rigor: the MCR\textsuperscript{2} coding rate.}
Daugman's $m_{\mathrm{eff}}$ assumes a binomial fit to the Hamming fraction. A
distribution-free count of independent coding directions is given by the maximal
coding-rate-reduction (MCR\textsuperscript{2}) rate \cite{YuEtAl20}: for unit feature
vectors stacked as columns of $Z \in \mathbb{R}^{p\times n}$ at quantization precision
$\varepsilon$,
\begin{equation}
R(Z,\varepsilon) \;=\; \tfrac12 \log\det\Bigl(I_p + \tfrac{p}{n\varepsilon^2} ZZ^\top\Bigr)
\quad \text{(nats)},
\end{equation}
with the per-class rate $R_c$ and rate-reduction $\Delta R = R - \sum_c (n_c/n) R_c$
formalizing the same ``spread-out yet within-class-compact'' target as
Theorem~\ref{thm:relaxation}; quantifying $R(Z,\varepsilon)$ and $\Delta R$ for the
Super-Bit, ortho-thermometer, and feature-head embeddings directly is left to future
work.

\begin{corollary}[The collision floor is a sphere-packing bound]
\label{cor:floor}
Since $q(0) = 1$, $\Pr[\text{accept}\mid H=0] = 1$ for every choice of $(w,t,T)$; hence
the probability of a zero-Hamming impostor collision, $p_0$, is a hard lower bound on the
per-record false-accept rate for any FLPSI parameterization over a fixed code, and it
propagates to $\mathrm{FAR}_{\mathrm{query}} = 1-(1-p_0)^N$ at 1:$N$ identification.
Bounding $p_0$ away from zero is a coding question: packing $N$ identity balls of
genuine intra-class radius $r_{\mathrm{gen}}$ into $\{0,1\}^d$ at pairwise minimum
distance $2r_{\mathrm{gen}}+1$ is constrained by classical binary-code bounds
(Hamming/sphere-packing $N \le 2^d/\sum_{k=0}^{r}\binom{d}{k}$, Singleton, Plotkin at
large relative distance). The binding quantity is the \emph{effective} dimension
$m_{\mathrm{eff}}$ of \S\ref{sec:effbits}, not the nominal $d$: a rank-limited code lives
in an effective space of size $2^{m_{\mathrm{eff}}}$, so raising $d$ without raising
$m_{\mathrm{eff}}$ leaves the packing budget --- and the floor --- essentially unchanged,
which is exactly what a dimension sweep on Super-Bit shows.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Immediate from $q(0)=1$ and the union/inclusion-exclusion bound over $N$ enrolled
identities for the 1:$N$ propagation; the sphere-packing, Singleton, and Plotkin bounds
are the classical coding-theory bounds applied to the effective code, restated here as a
corollary of the effective-dimension characterization rather than re-derived. \qed
\end{proof}

The floor is a diagnostic, not a verdict: the better codes in this paper drive the
measured collision count to zero on the held-out set (Table~\ref{tab:floor}); we recommend
reporting the $H=0$ count on held-out data as a standard diagnostic for any binariser.

\subsubsection{Lower-bound positioning}
\label{sec:lowerbound}

Chakrabarti and Regev prove an $\Omega(n)$ communication lower bound for
\textsc{Gap-Hamming-Distance} \cite{ChakrabartiRegev11}, but only under the hard promise
$|H-n/2| \ge \sqrt{n}$, a $\Theta(\sqrt{n})$ gap. Biometric matching operates in the
\emph{easy}, constant-fraction-gap regime instead: measured genuine mean Hamming distance
is roughly 7--17 bits against an impostor mean of roughly 47--64 bits at $d=128$ ---
a $\Theta(d)$ gap,
far outside the $\sqrt{n}$-promise hardness regime. The $\Omega(n)$ wall therefore does
not apply here, and there is genuine room for sub-linear communication; online
communication in our protocol is bounded instead by the cryptographic realization (OPRF
and OT cost scaling with $T$), which is exactly the quantity Corollary~\ref{cor:target}
lowers. Ghosh and Simkin separately show an $\Omega(t)$ communication lower bound for
threshold-PSI in the threshold parameter $t$ \cite{GhoshSimkin19}, a bound in a different
(set-intersection-size) model that is related but does not directly bound label-revealing
fuzzy PSI. We are not aware of a tight communication lower bound in the literature for
label-revealing FLPSI in the constant-fraction-gap regime; whether our $T$ is close to
optimal is therefore an open question, and we state it as such rather than claim
optimality.

\subsection{Two binariser instantiations}
\label{sec:binarisers}

Corollary~\ref{cor:target} and Theorem~\ref{thm:relaxation} give a single design target
-- balance the bits and decorrelate them, at a mean that keeps genuine Hamming distance
small. We give two binarisers realizing this target at different points on the
training-cost/accuracy trade-off.

\subsubsection{Training-free: the metric-aware ortho-thermometer}
\label{sec:ortho-thermo}

The ortho-thermometer code requires no training beyond the feature extractor already in
use. It replaces a single random-hyperplane sign per output dimension with a
per-dimension thermometer code built from block-orthonormalised projection directions and
population calibration statistics, so that Hamming distance under the sub-sampling
accept rule tracks the extractor's native angular (cosine) metric rather than a raw
per-axis threshold. Because it is training-free it is metric-\emph{aware} rather than
metric-\emph{learned}: it corrects the mismatch between an angular embedding space and a
Hamming-distance matcher without altering the network's weights or objective. Measured
against Corollary~\ref{cor:target}'s target, it reduces the impostor standard deviation
from $\sigma=20.11$ (Super-Bit) to $\sigma=14.60$ by improving balance and, to a lesser
extent, decorrelation, without a learning signal that explicitly penalizes covariance.

\subsubsection{Quantization-aware: the learned feature-head}
\label{sec:featurehead}

The feature-head instantiates Theorem~\ref{thm:relaxation}'s program directly as a
training objective on top of the wider convolutional feature representation (rather than
the 16-dimensional bottleneck feeding Super-Bit), realizing the target of
Corollary~\ref{cor:target} to a much tighter approximation:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Bit-balance loss} $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{bal}}$ penalizes deviation of each
  bit's empirical mean from $1/2$, enforcing $\mathbb{E}[D_i]=1/2$ (measured impostor
  mean $\to 64.1$, balance mean $\to 0.499$).
\item \textbf{Bit-decorrelation loss} $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{dec}}$ penalizes the
  off-diagonal norm $\lVert \hat C - \mathrm{diag}(\hat C)\rVert$ of the empirical bit
  covariance/correlation matrix, directly targeting the $\sum_{i\neq j}\mathrm{Cov}(D_i,D_j)$
  term of Theorem~\ref{thm:variance}.
\item \textbf{Genuine-closeness loss} $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{gen}}$ keeps
  $\mathbb{E}_{\mathrm{gen}}[H]$ small, maintaining feasibility (a usable $q_g$) while the
  other two terms drive the impostor law toward the balanced, decorrelated target.
\item \textbf{Straight-through estimator} $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{STE}}$ supplies a biased
  but trainable gradient surrogate through the non-differentiable sign binarisation, as
  flagged in the heuristic step of Theorem~\ref{thm:relaxation}'s proof.
\end{itemize}
The result is the $\sigma = 6.72$, $m_{\mathrm{eff}} \approx 91$ code used as the
paper's best operating point throughout \S\ref{sec:eval}; it is the closest of the three
codes to the balanced, decorrelated target that Theorem~\ref{thm:variance} and
Corollary~\ref{cor:target} identify as jointly accuracy- and communication-optimal, with
the caveats on exactness stated in Theorem~\ref{thm:relaxation}'s proof carried forward
honestly rather than dropped.

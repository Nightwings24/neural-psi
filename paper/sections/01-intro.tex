\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

In 2015 the United States Office of Personnel Management disclosed that attackers had
exfiltrated the records of 21.5 million people, including 5.6 million
fingerprints~\cite{Gootman16}. The passwords in that breach were reset within days. The
fingerprints could not be: a compromised biometric is compromised permanently. This
asymmetry is why ISO/IEC 24745~\cite{ISO24745} requires three properties of any stored
biometric reference --- \emph{irreversibility}, so the reference cannot be inverted to the
biometric; \emph{unlinkability}, so references for the same subject in different databases
cannot be correlated; and \emph{renewability}, so a compromised reference can be replaced
by a fresh one derived from the same trait. The first requirement is not paranoia: inverse
biometrics is now a demonstrated reconstruction threat against
representations once assumed one-way~\cite{GomezBarreroGalbally20}, so ``store the template
encrypted, or not at all'' is the only defensible default.

Systems that keep templates encrypted end-to-end address this directly, and homomorphic
encryption is the mature route. Blind-Touch~\cite{ChoiWooKim24} is the current reference
point: it evaluates the matching step under CKKS~\cite{CKKS17} so that the server never sees
a plaintext template. The guarantee is strong, and its costs are structural rather than
incidental. A 117\,MB Galois rotation key must be distributed to every client; each query
ciphertext is 856\,KB; and because the scheme packs 512 users into one ciphertext, the
database is organised around a slot-packing ceiling rather than around the workload.
Blind-Match~\cite{BlindMatch24} pushes $1{:}N$ throughput substantially, but it inherits the
same key-material and packing regime --- the cost is architectural to homomorphic matching,
not to any one instantiation of it.

Fuzzy-labelled private set intersection (FLPSI) offers a different trade. Introduced by
Uzun et al.\ for private real-time biometric search~\cite{UzunEtAl21}, FLPSI lets a client
learn only the \emph{label} attached to a server record within Hamming distance of its
query, at communication linear in the database size, with no key material of consequence
and no packing ceiling; the server learns nothing about the query beyond whether it
matched. We use Bui and Cong's vector ring-OLE construction~\cite{BuiCong25} as the concrete
efficient backend. This is the star primitive of this paper: a private matcher whose cost
structure is fundamentally lighter than a homomorphic one. But FLPSI matches
\emph{bit-strings} under Hamming distance, and a fingerprint is not a bit-string. The
\emph{binariser} --- the map from a learned embedding to a fixed-length binary code --- is
the interface that closes this gap, and in every neural-plus-crypto pipeline we are aware of
it is treated as an afterthought: a generic, off-the-shelf LSH map~\cite{Charikar02,JiEtAl12}
bolted on without regard for the matcher it feeds.

\paragraph{The central observation.} The binariser is not a neutral adapter. Under FLPSI's
sub-sample accept rule --- draw $T$ random $w$-position masks, accept once at least $t$ of
them collide --- a single statistic of the code, the distribution of Hamming distances to
\emph{impostor} codes, sets \emph{both} the false-accept rate at fixed $T$ \emph{and} the
number of sub-samples $T$ the protocol must exchange to reach a target operating point.
Accuracy and communication are therefore not independent knobs to be traded against one
another; they are two readings of a single design objective: push the impostor-Hamming
distribution toward the maximum-entropy $\mathrm{Binomial}(d,\tfrac12)$ that the protocol's
own parameter choice assumes. A code that is bit-balanced and pairwise-decorrelated over
impostors realises this objective; a code that is neither pays for it twice, once in error
rate and once in bandwidth.

We formalise this as a coupling law and a theorem (stated in full, with proof, in
\S\ref{sec:design}). The per-sub-sample collision probability at Hamming distance $H$ is
$q(H) = \binom{d-H}{w}/\binom{d}{w}$, and acceptance is governed by a binomial tail over $T$
independent masks; both the achievable (FAR, FRR) pair and the minimum $T$ needed to reach
it are functionals of the impostor Hamming law alone, and, at fixed mean $d/2$ enforced by
bit balance, of its \emph{variance} in particular. We show that among balanced codes, the
one whose impostor Hamming distribution matches $\mathrm{Binomial}(d,\tfrac12)$ --- minimum
impostor variance --- simultaneously minimises the number of sub-samples $T$ needed at any
target operating point and is error-optimal for the sub-sample accept rule; and that the
balance-and-decorrelation objective used to train a quantisation-aware feature head is
precisely a differentiable relaxation of this discrete optimality criterion. One better code
improves both readings at once.

\paragraph{What we build and find.} Neural-PSI is a two-party, label-only system: a Siamese
CNN runs client-side in plaintext, its embedding is binarised to a 128-bit code, and FLPSI
returns the matched label, never a similarity score. We realise the design objective above
with two binariser instantiations and validate the coupling law end-to-end through the real
FLPSI crypto binary, not a cost model. On SOCOFing, the co-designed code cuts equal-error
rate from 0.87\% to 0.17\% (about $5\times$; $\approx 3\times$ at medium difficulty,
$\approx 1.9\times$ at hard; non-overlapping identity-bootstrap 95\% confidence intervals
across the altered-difficulty ladder) \emph{and} cuts online communication by 45\% at a
matched operating point --- the same 128-bit backend, measured through the deployed Rust
crypto binary. The impostor standard deviation collapses from $20.11$ toward the ideal
$5.66$, and the code's effective bit count rises from roughly 10 to roughly 90 of its
nominal 128.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{description}\itemsep2pt
\item[C1.] A privacy-preserving fingerprint authentication design that replaces the
homomorphic matcher with fuzzy-labelled PSI: a Siamese CNN runs client-side in plaintext,
its embedding is binarised to a 128-bit code, and FLPSI returns the matched label rather
than a similarity score. This removes the 117\,MB rotation key, the 512-user packing
ceiling and the 3-server cluster; the server-side template is 16\,B
(\S\ref{sec:design}). The extractor architecture is inherited from
Blind-Touch~\cite{ChoiWooKim24}; the contribution is the composition and the label-only
disclosure model, not the network.

\item[C2.] A coupling theorem: the private matcher's error \emph{and} its online
communication are both functionals of one code statistic, the impostor Hamming
distribution, via the sub-sample accept rule; the balanced, minimum-impostor-variance code
(impostor Hamming $\to$ $\mathrm{Binomial}(d,\tfrac12)$) is simultaneously
communication-optimal and error-optimal for that rule, and the quantisation-aware feature
head's training objective is a differentiable relaxation of this discrete criterion. The
result is a \emph{matcher-agnostic design objective} for any sub-sample/threshold
fuzzy-PSI backend (\S\ref{sec:design}).

\item[C3.] Two binarisers realising the C2 objective as distinct actuators: a
training-free metric-aware bridge (orthonormal projections plus magnitude thresholds) that
corrects the distance \emph{geometry} the code preserves and drops in without retraining;
and a trained quantisation-aware feature head that produces a balanced, decorrelated
128-bit code from the extractor's high-dimensional features. Both are built from standard
learning-to-hash machinery; the novelty is the objective they are pointed at, not the
mechanism (\S\ref{sec:design}).

\item[C4.] End-to-end measurements through the real FLPSI binary, not a cost model: SOCOFing
EER $0.87\% \to 0.17\%$ ($\approx 5\times$; $\approx 3\times$ medium, $\approx 1.9\times$
hard; non-overlapping identity-bootstrap 95\% CIs across the full altered-difficulty
ladder), impostor $\sigma$ $20.11 \to 6.72$ (ideal $5.66$), effective bits
$\sim\!10 \to \sim\!90$ of 128, online communication $-45\%$ at a matched operating point
($4317 \to 2377$\,KB at $N{=}5000$), and real crypto-binary TAR $97.0\%$ / FAR $0.80\%$ at
$(w,t,T){=}(6,3,18)$. Across corpora the impostor-variance collapse, and hence the
communication benefit, is universal; the accuracy benefit is gap-dependent
(\S\ref{sec:eval}).

\item[C5.] A semi-honest security analysis --- ideal functionality, leakage function,
simulators for both corruptions and a hybrid sequence --- together with an assessment of
the stored 16\,B code against ISO/IEC 24745: a per-enrolment XOR mask recovers
unlinkability and renewability at zero accuracy cost (XOR preserves Hamming distance)
without delivering irreversibility, stated explicitly, alongside two attacks we do not
mitigate (\S\ref{sec:security})~\cite{ISO24745,GomezBarreroGalbally20}.
\end{description}

\paragraph{What we do not claim.} We do not claim to beat compressed CKKS on raw bandwidth:
Blind-Touch's 856\,KB per query is below our 2377\,KB; our $-45\%$ is measured against our
\emph{own} baseline, narrowing the gap from roughly $9\times$ to roughly $2.8\times$, not a
bandwidth win over homomorphic encryption. We do not claim the accuracy gain transfers to
every corpus: it scales with the \emph{quantisation gap} between the float embedding and any
128-bit code of it, and is marginal where the extractor, not the code, is the bottleneck, as
on harder real-world corpora; the communication gain, by contrast, is universal in our
measurements. SOCOFing is optimistic --- synthetic same-capture alterations --- and the
$\approx 5\times$ figure is a \emph{relative} gain on the same corpus, extractor and backend
as every baseline number we compare it to. We do not claim at-rest confidentiality against a
compromised server, nor security against malicious adversaries; our proofs are semi-honest.
And we are explicit about which of our two binarisers is which: the co-designed feature-head
code is \emph{trained}, on an identity-disjoint split; the metric-aware bridge is
training-free.

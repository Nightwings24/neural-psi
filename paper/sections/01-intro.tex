\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

When attackers took 5.6 million fingerprints in the 2015 U.S.\ Office of Personnel Management
breach~\cite{Gootman16}, the passwords were reset within days; the fingerprints could not be, because
a compromised biometric stays compromised. This is why ISO/IEC 24745~\cite{ISO24745} asks a stored
biometric reference to be \emph{irreversible} (not invertible to the trait), \emph{unlinkable} (not
correlatable across databases), and \emph{renewable} (replaceable from the same trait). Irreversibility
is not a formality: inverse biometrics now reconstructs traits from representations once assumed
one-way~\cite{GomezBarreroGalbally20}, so the only defensible default is to store the template
encrypted, or not at all.

Homomorphic encryption is the mature route. Blind-Touch~\cite{ChoiWooKim24} runs the matching step
under CKKS~\cite{CKKS17} so the server never sees a plaintext template, but the costs are structural: a
117\,MB Galois rotation key per client, an 856\,KB query ciphertext, and a database organised around a
512-user slot-packing ceiling. Blind-Match~\cite{BlindMatch24} raises $1{:}N$ throughput but keeps the
same regime, so the cost belongs to homomorphic matching itself. Fuzzy-labelled private set
intersection (FLPSI)~\cite{UzunEtAl21} offers a different trade: a client learns only the \emph{label}
on a server record within Hamming distance of its query, at communication linear in $N$, with no key
material of consequence and no packing ceiling, and the server learns only whether a match occurred.
We use Bui and Cong's vector ring-OLE backend~\cite{BuiCong25}. But FLPSI matches bit-strings under
Hamming distance, and a fingerprint is not a bit-string: the \emph{binariser} from embedding to
fixed-length code closes that gap, and in the pipelines we know of it is an afterthought, a generic
LSH map~\cite{Charikar02,JiEtAl12} bolted on without regard for the matcher it feeds.

\paragraph{The central observation.} The binariser is not a neutral adapter. Under FLPSI's sub-sample
accept rule (draw $T$ random $w$-position masks, accept once at least $t$ collide), one statistic of
the code, the distribution of Hamming distances to \emph{impostor} codes, sets both the false-accept
rate at fixed $T$ and the number of sub-samples $T$ the protocol must exchange to reach a target.
Accuracy and communication are two readings of one objective: push the impostor-Hamming distribution
toward the maximum-entropy $\mathrm{Binomial}(d,\tfrac12)$ the protocol's parameter choice assumes. We
formalise this in \S\ref{sec:design}: the per-sub-sample collision probability is $q(H) =
\binom{d-H}{w}/\binom{d}{w}$, acceptance is a binomial tail over $T$ masks, and both the achievable
(FAR, FRR) pair and the minimum $T$ are functionals of the impostor Hamming law, and at the fixed mean
$d/2$ that balance enforces, of its variance. Among balanced codes the minimum-variance one (impostor
law $\to \mathrm{Binomial}(d,\tfrac12)$) minimises $T$ at any target and is error-optimal for the
accept rule, and the balance-and-decorrelation objective training a quantisation-aware feature head is
a differentiable relaxation of that discrete criterion. One better code improves both readings.

We realise the objective with two binarisers and validate the coupling law end to end through the real
FLPSI crypto binary, not a cost model. On SOCOFing the co-designed code cuts EER from $0.87\%$ to
$0.17\%$ (about $5\times$; non-overlapping identity-bootstrap 95\% CIs across the difficulty ladder)
and online communication by $45\%$ at a matched operating point on the same 128-bit backend; the
impostor standard deviation collapses from $20.11$ toward the ideal $5.66$ and effective bits rise from
roughly 10 to roughly 90 of 128.

\paragraph{Contributions.} \textbf{(C1)} A privacy-preserving fingerprint design that replaces the
homomorphic matcher with fuzzy-labelled PSI (client-side plaintext Siamese CNN, 128-bit code, FLPSI
returning the matched label), removing the 117\,MB rotation key, the 512-user packing ceiling, and the
3-server cluster for a 16\,B template; the extractor is inherited from Blind-Touch~\cite{ChoiWooKim24},
so the contribution is the composition and the label-only disclosure model (\S\ref{sec:design}).
\textbf{(C2)} A coupling theorem: the matcher's error and online communication are both functionals of
one statistic, the impostor Hamming distribution, and the balanced, minimum-variance code is at once
communication- and error-optimal for the accept rule, with the feature head's objective a
differentiable relaxation of it, giving a matcher-agnostic design objective for any
sub-sample/threshold fuzzy-PSI backend. \textbf{(C3)} Two binarisers for that objective, a training-free
metric-aware bridge and a trained quantisation-aware feature head, both from standard
learning-to-hash machinery with the objective they target being what is new. \textbf{(C4)} End-to-end
measurements through the real FLPSI binary: SOCOFing EER $0.87\% \to 0.17\%$ (non-overlapping 95\% CIs),
impostor $\sigma$ $20.11 \to 6.72$ (ideal $5.66$), effective bits $\sim\!10 \to \sim\!90$, communication
$-45\%$ ($4317 \to 2377$\,KB at $N{=}5000$), and real crypto-binary TAR $97.0\%$ / FAR $0.80\%$ at
$(6,3,18)$; the impostor-variance collapse (hence communication benefit) is universal across corpora
while the accuracy benefit is gap-dependent (\S\ref{sec:eval}). \textbf{(C5)} A semi-honest security
analysis and an assessment of the 16\,B stored code against ISO/IEC 24745: a per-enrolment XOR mask
recovers unlinkability and renewability at zero accuracy cost but not irreversibility, which we state
plainly, alongside two attacks we do not mitigate
(\S\ref{sec:security})~\cite{ISO24745,GomezBarreroGalbally20}.

\paragraph{What we do not claim.} We do not beat compressed CKKS on raw bandwidth: Blind-Touch's
856\,KB per query is below our 2377\,KB, and the $-45\%$ narrows our own gap from $\sim9\times$ to
$\sim2.8\times$ rather than reversing it. The accuracy gain does not transfer to every corpus: it scales
with the quantisation gap between the float embedding and any 128-bit code of it, and is marginal where
the extractor, not the code, is the bottleneck, though the communication gain is universal in our
measurements. SOCOFing is optimistic (synthetic same-capture alterations), the $\approx 5\times$ is a
\emph{relative} gain on one corpus/extractor/backend, and the proofs are semi-honest (no at-rest or
malicious-adversary security).

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

\paragraph{Single high-fidelity corpus.} The headline EER ladder (\S\ref{sec:params},
\S\ref{sec:eval}) is measured on SOCOFing, whose probes are synthetic degradations
(obliteration, rotation, z-cut) of the \emph{same} capture rather than independent acquisitions
across sensors or sessions. This is a favourable, same-sensor setting, and the generality results
on PolyU and FVC2002 confirm it is optimistic: cross-sensor transfer sits at chance
(\S\ref{sec:eval}), and the co-designed code's accuracy gain shrinks or disappears wherever the
underlying features are already the bottleneck. SOCOFing should be read as an upper bound on what
this design achieves elsewhere, not as a representative deployment corpus.

\paragraph{The accuracy gain is gap-dependent, and that is the theory working, not failing.}
Theorem~4.3 (\S\ref{sec:design}) says the feature-head code recovers accuracy already present in
the underlying features but lost to quantization; it cannot manufacture discriminability the
extractor never had. Where the floating-point features are much better than the 16-dimensional
code they replace (SOCOFing, conv EER $0.16\%$ against Super-Bit's $1.89\%$), closing that gap is
dramatic. Where they are not --- PolyU-contact and FVC2002 Db4, where the 16-D embedding is
already close to the float ceiling --- the feature-head code is a wash or a regression relative to
the metric-aware bridge. We read this as confirmation of the mechanism (accuracy gain tracks the
quantization gap exactly as \S\ref{sec:design} predicts), not as a limitation of the method
independent of the theory, but it does mean the reported gains are corpus- and extractor-specific
and should not be extrapolated to settings with no such gap.

\paragraph{Communication: a trade-off against Blind-Touch's compressed CKKS, not a win.} The
co-designed code lowers our own communication by $45\%$ at a matched operating point
(\S\ref{sec:comms}), but this narrows a gap rather than closing it: at $N=5{,}000$ our matched
point costs $2377$\,KB against Blind-Touch's $856$\,KB of homomorphically compressed CKKS
ciphertext~\cite{ChoiWooKim24}. FLPSI pays this in exchange for eliminating Blind-Touch's $117$\,MB
Galois rotation key and its slot-packing ceiling, and for a $16$-byte server-side template against
$1.67$\,KB; the two designs sit on different points of a communication/key-material/storage
trade-off, and this paper does not claim to dominate on all three simultaneously. Reporting
communication alone without this context would overstate the result.

\paragraph{Semi-honest security only.} The security argument (\S\ref{sec:security}) simulates a
semi-honest server and an honest-but-curious client; a malicious client that deviates from the
protocol or supplies a $\xt$ with no binding to a live finger is out of scope, and sensor
attestation is load-bearing rather than a hardening detail. The vector ring-OLE backend targeted
for the crypto realization (Bui--Cong~\cite{BuiCong25}) is likewise a semi-honest construction;
extending either side to malicious security is future work, not a gap in the present analysis.

\paragraph{Template irreversibility is a protocol property, not a binariser property.} As
\S\ref{sec:design} states explicitly, the feature-head's sign map is an over-determined,
$8\times$-oversampled measurement of a low-dimensional embedding and must not be treated as
one-way: the argument that made this claim for the Super-Bit map (compressed-sensing recovery
angle) is specific to that $128{\times}16$ linear map and does not transfer to the learned
feature-head. We do not claim the feature-head code is one-way either. Confidentiality of the
stored code rests on the protocol --- the server is the mOPRF sender and never observes $\xt$ or a
plaintext sub-sample of it (\S\ref{sec:security}) --- and full at-rest confidentiality against a
compromised server remains out of scope, exactly as for the naïve code.

\paragraph{The feature-head code is trained; the round-1 bridge is not.} The metric-aware bridge
of round one is a fixed, public, training-free map, published alongside the model like a
batch-normalisation statistic. The feature-head code of round two is trained end-to-end against
the balance/decorrelation/genuine-closeness objective of Theorem~4.3, on an identity-disjoint
split with the reported epoch selected on a held-out validation split. This buys the larger
accuracy and communication gains but introduces a training pipeline, a held-out validation
protocol, and a straight-through gradient estimator whose optimization is non-convex even though
the population objective it approximates is convex in the code's second moments
(\S\ref{sec:design}, Theorem~4.3, item 2). We do not claim the training recovers a global optimum,
only that it reliably moves the measured impostor law toward the uniform ideal.
